## CoreWeave's Data Center Financing Structure    *22:02*

<span class="paragraph-timestamp" data-ts="22:06">22:06</span> **Chester Roh** Another thing that happened in the meantime involves CoreWeave, which is central to this story. Its corporate structure is complicated. I once heard CoreWeave’s CEO explain it somewhere: they create a box, put a 15-year data center contract into it, then buy equipment and put that in, and add a customer contract. That lasts three years. Then, to support all that, they raise debt. They issue five-year bonds and keep it all within that one box. Hyperscalers usually buy those boxes. Anthropic, OpenAI, and a third company reportedly account for nearly 70% of the total contracted volume. They buy the boxes, and some bonds even have guarantees from the buyers. After they’ve lowered the rate, they deduct the expenses and financing costs, and what remains is CoreWeave’s profit. The business is structured so those profits and risks accumulate at the parent company, CoreWeave itself. So the CoreWeave parent company doesn’t operate the entire IDC on its own. Below it are separate entities, like subsidiaries, that hold those contracts as distinct businesses.
