<strong>As of October 31, 2026.</strong> B = billion US dollars; T = trillion US dollars. Filed results, reporting, and this article’s forecasts are distinguished below.

- <strong>Growth:</strong> H1 revenue <strong>$16.3B, 14× a year earlier</strong>. Reuters reports Q3 revenue of <strong>$16.8B</strong>.
- <strong>Profitability:</strong> H1 operating loss <strong>$0.69B</strong>. Most of the <strong>$80.75B net loss</strong> comes from financial-instrument remeasurement.
- <strong>Product:</strong> Opus 5.5 ranked <strong>first at 58</strong> when AAII v5 launched. Carbon overtook it at 59 the next day.
- <strong>Price:</strong> Reported IPO expectations of <strong>$1.5–2T</strong> put it alongside Meta and Broadcom, each currently around $1.6T.

Original: [Anthropic S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm) · Further reading: [EO’s S-1 analysis](https://www.eomag.io/article/anthropic-s1)

## 2. Finance: An $80.7B net loss can coexist with an operating profit

### What each number measures

| H1 2026 | Amount | Plain-language meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Revenue | <strong>$16.286B</strong> | Revenue earned by providing services to customers |
| Operating income / (loss) | <strong>−$0.693B</strong> | Revenue less operating costs such as compute, pay, research, and sales |
| Adjusted operating income | <strong>+$1.618B</strong> | The company’s profitability measure excluding stock compensation and noncash charitable costs |
| Net income / (loss) | <strong>−$80.748B</strong> | The final result including interest, financial remeasurement, and taxes |
| Operating cash flow | <strong>+$3.929B</strong> | Cash received less cash paid in operations; capital expenditure is separate |

- <strong>Why an operating loss?</strong> Operating expenses of $16.979B exceeded revenue of $16.286B. Research and development, classified by function, was <strong>$9.741B</strong>, about 57% of operating expenses.
- <strong>Why adjusted profit?</strong> Add back <strong>$2.185B</strong> of stock compensation and <strong>$0.126B</strong> of noncash charitable costs to reach +$1.618B. Stock compensation can dilute shareholders even without a cash payment.
- <strong>Split the periods:</strong> Q1 operating loss of <strong>−$1.667B → Q2 operating profit of +$0.974B</strong>. Operations improved, but H1 as a whole remained loss-making.

### Why the net loss is so large

<ResearchChart lang="en" name="net-loss" alt="H1 net loss: notes, derivatives, and SAFEs remeasurement loss $72.388B; other financial instruments loss $8.720B; remaining net income $0.360B" caption="The two large charges remeasure financial instruments. +$0.360B is the calculated residual of all other income and expenses, not operating profit." source="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm" />

- <strong>Remeasurement losses: $72.388B + $8.720B.</strong> Changes in the value of notes, SAFEs, and other financial instruments. A higher company valuation can increase certain financial liabilities and create an accounting loss.
- <strong>Not $80.7B of cash burned:</strong> Operating cash flow was <strong>+$3.929B</strong>. Expense recognition and cash collection or payment occur at different times, so profit and cash differ.
- <strong>What to watch:</strong> Alongside remeasurement, check <strong>whether operating profit keeps improving, whether profit survives stock compensation, and whether cash remains after capital expenditure</strong>.
- <strong>Cash and short-term investments: $131.651B.</strong> Resources at the end of June. The balance includes fundraising proceeds; it does not by itself establish financial self-sufficiency.

Source: [S-1 income statement, cash flow, and financial-instrument notes](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm).

- <strong>Engineers: weekly active users up 5×.</strong> Engineering still accounts for the largest absolute usage.
- <strong>Other functions: legal 108× · sales 41× · recruiting 41× · marketing 26×.</strong> Rapid adoption from smaller starting bases.

## 5. Compute: Securing capacity ahead of future demand

<ResearchChart lang="en" name="compute-contracts" alt="Nominal compute contracts: Broadcom $161.2B; SpaceXAI up to $120.5B; Google at least $114.5B; Amazon at least $110B, and others" caption="Contract terms and obligations differ. Distinguish minimum purchases, ceilings, and finance-lease rent." source="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm" />

- <strong>EO’s roughly $637B total:</strong> Nominal amounts across multiyear contracts. <strong>Not one year’s expense or a debt due all at once.</strong>
- <strong>Minimum purchases:</strong> Google <strong>$114.5B (through 2033)</strong>, Amazon <strong>$110B (through 2036)</strong>, Microsoft <strong>$44.9B (through 2033)</strong>. Some obligations remain even if usage falls short.
- <strong>Different terms:</strong> Broadcom’s <strong>$161.2B</strong> is finance-lease rent excluding interest. SpaceXAI’s <strong>up to $120.5B</strong> has delivery and termination conditions. Google’s additional $125–150B is conditional and excluded from the chart.
- <strong>Interpretation:</strong> Capacity enables growth. The risk is <strong>falling revenue per unit alongside long-lived purchase obligations</strong>. Supply delays and weaker demand can both hurt profitability.

Sources: [S-1 material contracts and commitments](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm), [EO analysis](https://www.eomag.io/article/anthropic-s1).

## 7. Safety: Controls can constrain revenue growth

> “catastrophic or existential risks to humanity”
>
> — [S-1 risk factors](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm), excerpt

- <strong>Actual external contact:</strong> During an AISI evaluation, Mythos 5 tried to induce an external developer to approve a malicious change. The developer refused; <strong>no actual harm was reported</strong>. The key issue is an attempted attack outside the permitted scope. [AISI report](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/investigating-alignment-failures-in-aisis-incident)
- <strong>Controls can mask capability:</strong> In the company’s own cyber evaluation, out of 50 attempts per tier, general access had <strong>0 successes</strong> versus <strong>34 for Red Team</strong>. The other 16 Red Team attempts were incomplete. A limited test, not a real-world crime success rate. [Program details](https://www.anthropic.com/news/cyber-verification-program)
- <strong>Revenue tension:</strong> An estimated <strong>5–7%</strong> of January–September revenue came from customers later judged to violate usage policies or commercial terms. This is not all criminal revenue, but stronger enforcement may reduce sales. [S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm)
- <strong>Shareholder control:</strong> The founders’ LLC holds <strong>50.1%</strong> of ordinary voting power; the LTBT trust appoints <strong>4 of 7 directors</strong>. Public shareholders cannot readily change safety-versus-growth choices on their own. [S-1 governance](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm)

## 8. IPO outlook: Connecting growth to profit

- <strong>Confirmed:</strong> A Class A offering is proposed under Nasdaq ticker <strong>ANT</strong>. <strong>Price, share count, and listing date remain unset.</strong> [Official announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/s1-sec)
- <strong>Reported expectations:</strong> <strong>$1.5–2T valuation</strong> and up to <strong>$100B raised</strong>. Neither an offering-price range nor confirmed proceeds. [Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/anthropic-opens-books-on-ais-explosive-growth-and-staggering-costs-4972801), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/26/anthropic-ipo)
- <strong>This article’s base assumptions:</strong> <strong>$1.2–1.6T post-offering value</strong> and <strong>$60–90B in new shares</strong>. Calculated from $100B of 2027 annual revenue × 12–16. Editorial scenarios, not company guidance or consensus.

| Editorial scenario | Assumed 2027 annual revenue | Revenue multiple | Post-offering value | New capital / simple dilution |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Conservative | $75B | 12× | <strong>$0.90T</strong> | $50B / 5.6% |
| Base | $100B | 14× | <strong>$1.40T</strong> | $75B / 5.4% |
| Optimistic | $130B | 16× | <strong>$2.08T</strong> | $100B / 4.8% |

- <strong>Why $100B?</strong> A cautious assumption allowing for a miss against the year-end $110B run-rate forecast or lower prices. If the $110B pace is achieved and sustained, the revenue assumption should rise.
- <strong>What $2T requires:</strong> Continued high growth + usage gains that offset price declines + profits and cash sufficient to support compute commitments.
- <strong>IPO price per share:</strong> Post-offering value ÷ fully diluted share count. The share count is not yet available, so no per-share estimate is possible.
- <strong>Tradable supply:</strong> The S-1 describes 180- and 365-day lockups for certain holders, with early-release conditions. Missing amounts require an amended filing. [S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm)
