<h2 id="ai-progress">1. AI progress — What a Ranking Measures Matters More</h2>

<ResourceLink
  url="https://www.stateof.ai/2026-explore/research"
  title="AI progress — Topic Analysis"

  description="The original analysis of frontier model competition, benchmark limitations, and research automation."

### Intelligence rankings and people’s preferred answers have different leaders

<Figure
  src="/articles/state-of-ai-2026/frontier-race.png"
  alt="Comparison chart: Claude Opus 5.5 leads the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 58, with GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 4 Argon at 53. Gemini 4 Argon leads Arena at 1525."
  caption="State of AI Report 2026, slide 5. The Intelligence Index is on the left; user preference rankings are on the right. Scores on the two axes cannot be directly compared."
/>

- **The report describes a three-way race between Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google.** Claude Opus 5.5 scores 58 on Artificial Analysis, while GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 4 Argon each score 53.
- **The leader changes when people’s preferences are the measure.** Argon leads Arena at 1525, with Claude models clustered around 1505. At the time of the report, Argon was available only to selected users.

[Source: slide 5 · PDF page 6](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=6)

### Established tests are saturating, while partial credit can obscure completion

<Figure
  src="/articles/state-of-ai-2026/benchmark-ceilings.png"
  alt="Trend chart showing the best scores on math, science, and coding benchmarks featured in the previous report rapidly approaching their ceilings."
  caption="State of AI Report 2026, slide 37. As established tests saturate, harder tasks and clearer completion criteria become necessary."
/>

<Figure
  src="/articles/state-of-ai-2026/task-completion.png"
  alt="The gap between partial credit and completion: GPT-5.6 Sol scores 87.9 with 20.6% completion on FrontierChallenge; Opus 5 scores 77.7% with 44.3% completion on OSWorld 2.0."
  caption="State of AI Report 2026, slide 42. Doing parts of a task well can differ greatly from completing the work through final submission."
/>

- **Across 97 scientific workflows in FrontierChallenge,** GPT-5.6 Sol scored **87.9/100** for meeting individual requirements but fully completed **20.6%** of the tasks.
- **Across 108 computer tasks in OSWorld 2.0,** Opus 5 earned **77.7%** partial credit and completed **44.3%**. A missed approval or an unsubmitted form can leave otherwise useful work unfinished.
- **METR’s “50% time horizon”** is the human-expert duration of tasks an agent can complete with a 50% probability. It does not mean an agent can safely be left unattended for that length of time. The evaluation discussed in the report includes only five tasks taking humans more than 16 hours, making estimates at the upper end unreliable.

[Source: slide 37 · PDF page 38](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=38) · [Slides 42–43 begin at PDF page 43](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=43)

### AI is helping build AI, while choosing research directions remains a challenge

- **Anthropic’s internal metric:** Claude’s share of measured model R&D rose from **under 1% in February 2026 to 26% in August**. Humans set the tasks and supervise execution.
- **Running experiments and choosing good research are different capabilities.** In the report’s evaluation using unpublished NeurIPS paper tasks, agents received six days, GPUs, and $3,000 in API credits. Opus 4.8 completed the engineering, but its two papers received rejection scores of 2/6 and 1/6.

[Anthropic’s original R&D measurement analysis](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development) · [Research task evaluation: slide 17 · PDF page 18](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=18)

> Discussion question: How does our organization distinguish a plausible intermediate result from work that has passed final review?

<h2 id="ai-agents">2. AI agents — Tools and Context Change What the Same Model Can Do</h2>

<ResourceLink
  url="https://www.stateof.ai/2026-explore/agents"
  title="AI agents — Topic Analysis"
  domain="stateof.ai"
  description="Harnesses, memory, task routing, the limits of long-running work, and adoption beyond developers."
/>

### Holding the model fixed, the success rate rose by 13 percentage points

A **harness** is the software around a model that supplies tools, manages context and task state, and connects failures to feedback and retries.

| GLM-5.1 agent setup | Tasks resolved |
| --- | --- |
| Minimal | 52.5% |
| Improved | 56.5% |
| Full | 65.5% |

- The study held **model weights fixed** and changed only tools, context, and feedback. It used **100 SWE-bench Verified tasks**, reporting mean pass@1 across two runs.
- **The increase from 52.5% to 65.5% is 13 percentage points**, or about 25% relative to the starting score. It does not guarantee the same improvement on every kind of work.

[Original study · Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23950v1) · [Slide 7 · PDF page 8](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=8)

### Choosing the right setup for the problem

<Figure
  src="/articles/state-of-ai-2026/agent-routing.png"
  alt="Venn diagrams showing the overlapping and different problems solved by two specialized harnesses. With Gemini 3 Flash, the router reaches 62%, Meta-Harness 46%, and the branches’ potential union 67%."
  caption="State of AI Report 2026, slide 8. Routing helps because the two setups solve different problems. Circle areas are schematic."
/>

- **In a math evaluation with Gemini 3 Flash held fixed,** a router choosing between specialized harnesses reached **62%**, versus **46%** for Meta-Harness. Either branch could solve **67%** of the problems in total, leaving room to improve the routing decision.
- **Memory and skills** let an agent carry information from earlier work into later tasks without retraining the model. Storing records alone does not establish learning; the benefit needs to be checked on repeated tasks and verifiable outcomes.
- **As models improve, existing scaffolding needs to be retested.** The report describes Claude Code removing 80% of its system prompt for advanced models without a measurable decline on coding evaluations.

[Source: slide 8 · PDF page 9](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=9) · [Slide 10 · PDF page 11](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=11) · [Slide 14 · PDF page 15](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=15)

### Adoption is spreading beyond developers, while long-term judgment remains a separate challenge

<Figure
  src="/articles/state-of-ai-2026/agent-adoption.png"
  alt="Slide showing Codex adoption beyond developers alongside its share of output tokens across departments inside OpenAI."
  caption="State of AI Report 2026, slide 99. Growth among organizational users on the left and token shares inside OpenAI on the right have different populations and denominators."
/>

- OpenAI’s adoption study found **rapid growth from small starting points among non-developers**, including legal, sales, recruiting, and marketing. A higher growth multiple does not establish a larger current user base.
- **In KellyBench’s simulated betting market,** all 12 models lost money on average across five runs, and six went bankrupt at least once.
- **In E-CommerceBench’s simulated stores,** 16 of 18 models showed no clear improvement in bargaining on repeat purchases. Performance needs to include fraud exposure, losses, and learning from experience alongside returns. Neither simulation measures actual business profitability.

[OpenAI adoption study](https://openai.com/index/how-agents-are-transforming-work/) · [Slide 99 · PDF page 100](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=100) · [Long-running evaluations: slides 44–45 begin at PDF page 45](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=45)

> Discussion question: Before paying for a more expensive model, which should we change first: tools, task context, review, or retries?

<h2 id="compute">3. Compute &amp; infrastructure — Turning Investment Commitments into Usable Capacity</h2>

<ResourceLink
  url="https://www.stateof.ai/2026-explore/compute"
  title="Compute & infrastructure — Topic Analysis"
  domain="stateof.ai"
  description="Financing, live power capacity, agent runtime environments, community acceptance, and supply bottlenecks."
/>

### $733B in annual capital expenditure, with different scopes in the US and Europe

- **Four US hyperscalers guided to about $733B in total capital expenditure for 2026.** The entire amount is not dedicated to AI.
- **The EU AI Gigafactories initiative has an initial €1B contribution,** within a program seeking up to €10B in public funding and at least €20B in private funding. It does not represent all European AI investment and has a different scope from US companies’ total annual CAPEX.
- NVIDIA’s partnership with six financial institutions **aims to mobilize more than $500B** for AI infrastructure. That is a target, rather than cash already deployed or completed data centers.

[Report slide 134 · PDF page 135](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=135) · [European Commission gigafactories call](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/eu-launches-ai-gigafactories-call-boost-europes-computing-capacity-and-unlock-more-eu30-billion)

### About 0.9 GW is live against 5.6 GW of contracted power

<Figure
  src="/articles/state-of-ai-2026/contracted-vs-live-power.png"
  alt="Contracted versus live AI power capacity among Bitcoin miners moving into AI infrastructure: about 5.6 GW contracted and about 900 MW live in total."
  caption="State of AI Report 2026, slide 123. AI power capacity among the miners tracked by the report, as of Q2 2026. This is not a utilization rate for the entire AI data center market."
/>

- **Power contracts → permits → construction → power delivery → equipment installation:** customers can run workloads only once the chain is complete. Existing mining sites and grid connections still require additional construction for AI facilities.
- **CoreWeave is a separate example:** it reported **1.5 GW** of live power at the end of Q2 and **4.2 GW** of contracted power as of August 11. The dates differ, and the figures cannot be added together as total live capacity.
- **Agent infrastructure extends beyond GPUs.** MAI-Thinking-1 assigned 4,096 of its 4,864 GB200s to inference that generated training experience. CPUs, memory, and sandboxes that support pausing and resuming work are also part of the cost.

[Slide 123 · PDF page 124](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=124) · [Training infrastructure: slide 26 · PDF page 27](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=27)

### Beyond funding and chips: community consent and future payment obligations

<Figure
  src="/articles/state-of-ai-2026/data-center-opposition.png"
  alt="Gallup survey: 71% oppose building a local AI data center, compared with 53% opposing a nuclear power plant. AI data center opposition is 48% strongly opposed and 23% somewhat opposed."
  caption="State of AI Report 2026, slide 188. Gallup’s March 2026 US survey found 71% opposed to a local AI data center."
/>

- **Gallup:** **71%** opposed a local AI data center and **53%** opposed a nuclear power plant. The survey asks residents about their preferences regarding different types of facilities.
- **Data Center Watch:** local opposition delayed or blocked at least **45 projects** representing about **$68B** in planned investment during Q2 2026. This does not mean all that investment was canceled or became a realized loss.
- **The financing questions are growing too.** The Morgan Stanley analysis cited in the report puts off-balance-sheet commitments and credit support across seven hyperscaler and semiconductor companies at **more than $3T**. Most are future purchase and lease commitments; they are not all current borrowings or immediate losses.

[Gallup original survey](https://news.gallup.com/poll/709772/americans-oppose-data-centers-area.aspx) · [Data Center Watch Q2](https://www.datacenterwatch.org/q2-2026) · [Slide 188 · PDF page 189](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=189) · [Financial commitments: slide 127 · PDF page 128](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=128)

> Discussion question: Where is AI infrastructure competitiveness being decided: securing GPUs, securing power, finishing construction, or gaining community acceptance?

<h2 id="business">4. AI in business — Intelligence Gets Cheaper as Usage and Revenue Grow</h2>

<ResourceLink
  url="https://www.stateof.ai/2026-explore/business"
  title="AI in business — Topic Analysis"
  domain="stateof.ai"
  description="Inference costs and revenue growth, AI-native companies, task-specific model optimization, and changes in employment."
/>

### The cost of reaching the same score falls by about 47% per quarter

<Figure
  src="/articles/state-of-ai-2026/inference-cost.png"
  alt="Declining inference costs for reaching the same benchmark score. Epoch AI estimates an average quarterly decline of 47%, or about a 13-fold annual reduction."
  caption="State of AI Report 2026, slide 109. The trend tracks the minimum cost of achieving a fixed level of performance, rather than the decline in a company’s total AI spending."
/>

- Across five benchmarks since 2023, Epoch AI estimates that **the cost of reaching the same score has fallen by an average of 47% per quarter**, reaching about **one-thirteenth of the cost per year**.
- **The bill can grow even as the unit price falls.** Agents taking on longer tasks and making repeated model calls increase usage, retries, and review costs.

[Epoch AI analysis · September 22, 2026](https://epoch.ai/publications/the-plunging-price-of-thought) · [Slide 109 · PDF page 110](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=110)

### Two frontier companies reach about $105B in combined annualized revenue

<Figure
  src="/articles/state-of-ai-2026/frontier-revenue.png"
  alt="Chart showing OpenAI and Anthropic’s combined reported annualized revenue growing from about $30B in early 2026 to about $105B by late summer."
  caption="State of AI Report 2026, slide 82. Reported annualized revenue covers both companies’ entire businesses, rather than API inference revenue alone."
/>

- **About $30B in early 2026 → about $105B by late summer.** These annualized figures cover company-wide business, including subscriptions, coding products, and APIs.
- **Annualized revenue, or run rate,** extrapolates the revenue pace at a particular moment over a year. It is not already realized annual revenue or profit.
- **Spending intensity varies greatly.** In Ramp’s August sample, median monthly AI spending per employee was **$7,205** among the top 1% of AI-spending companies, compared with **$12.50** across all companies. Ramp’s customer sample cannot be generalized to every company worldwide.

[Slide 82 · PDF page 83](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=83) · [Spending concentration: slide 95 · PDF page 96](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=96)

### Companies built around AI and companies that add AI features

<Figure
  src="/articles/state-of-ai-2026/ai-native-growth.png"
  alt="Standard Metrics preliminary Q2 2026 data: 75th-percentile growth is 256% for AI-native companies versus 90% for AI-enabled companies at $1M–$20M annualized revenue, and 172% versus 53% above $20M."
  caption="State of AI Report 2026, slide 93. These are 75th-percentile growth rates, not averages, and the comparison does not establish a causal effect from adopting AI."
/>

| Annualized revenue band | AI-native year-over-year growth · 75th percentile | Companies adding AI features · 75th percentile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $1M–$20M | 256% | 90% |
| Above $20M | 172% | 53% |

- This is a **preliminary Q2 2026 sample**. Differences in company age, market, and access to investment prevent a conclusion that adopting AI triples growth.
- **Data from specialized work can become a competitive advantage.** Harvey reports that a post-trained GLM-5.2 reduced its legal review table cost per cell by **54.8% versus Sonnet 5**. Its internal answer-quality metric was **0.903, versus Fable 5’s 0.867**. This is a result on that legal task evaluation, rather than a claim of general superiority.
- **Employment is moving in different directions.** The report discusses hiring growth among high AI-spending companies alongside slower hiring in some entry-level and exposed occupations. Company-level correlations and occupation-level changes cannot be combined to calculate AI’s net employment effect.

[Growth rates: slide 93 · PDF page 94](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=94) · [Specialized models: slide 113 · PDF page 114](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=114) · [Employment: slides 103–106 begin at PDF page 104](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=104)

> Discussion question: Where does our product’s defensibility come from: its interface and features, workflow context, customer data, or the ability to evaluate correct answers?

<h2 id="policy">5. Policy &amp; geopolitics — AI Sovereignty Depends on Chips, Models, Operations, and Access</h2>

<ResourceLink
  url="https://www.stateof.ai/2026-explore/policy"
  title="Policy & geopolitics — Topic Analysis"
  domain="stateof.ai"
  description="Model access controls, sovereign AI investment and Korea’s strategy, public compute, regulation, and security."
/>

### Policy decisions can change access to a model

- **June 12:** according to Anthropic, US export guidance applied to Fable 5 and Mythos 5; in its response, the company suspended both models for all users.
- **June 30 → July 1:** after announcing that the controls had been lifted, the company announced restored access in a July 1 update. That does not mean availability became identical across all products, clouds, and customers.

[Anthropic’s export guidance announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access) · [Access restoration announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/redeploying-fable-5)

### About $138B in sovereign AI pledges, at different stages of delivery

<Figure
  src="/articles/state-of-ai-2026/sovereign-ai-map.png"
  alt="Map showing about $138B pledged to selected sovereign AI programs in Canada, the UK, the EU, the US, China, Japan, Korea, India, the Middle East, and elsewhere, distinguished by procurement, capital formation, and operational stage."
  caption="State of AI Report 2026, slide 170. About $138B in total pledges to selected programs. Time periods, project scopes, and delivery stages differ; this is not total actual spending."
/>

- The report’s **roughly $138B** and CNAS’s **67 countries, 184 projects, and about $84B in disclosed budgets** cover different sets of countries and projects. They should neither be treated as conflicting estimates of the same population nor added together.
- NVIDIA’s separately reported **more than $30B in fiscal 2026 sovereign AI revenue** uses another accounting basis and scope. It cannot be added to national program pledges.
- **The author’s argument:** domestic data centers alone do not provide control over models. Sovereignty requires researchers, energy, compute, and the continuing ability to build the next generation of models.

[CNAS Sovereign AI Index](https://interactives.cnas.org/reports/sovereign-ai-index/) · [Slides 169–170 begin at PDF page 170](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=170)

### Korea appears as a case of connecting models, talent, compute, and demand

<Figure
  src="/articles/state-of-ai-2026/korea-ai-strategy.png"
  alt="Infographic organizing Korea’s AI strategy into six elements: domestic models, engineering talent, inference capacity, public demand, universal access, and community opposition. It shows a 2026 AI budget of KRW 9.9T and a target of at least 50,000 government-led GPUs by 2028."
  caption="State of AI Report 2026, slide 172. The report’s interpretation of Korea’s strategy. Budgets, procurement contracts, and future GPU targets need to be distinguished from completed results."
/>

- The report highlights Korea’s **KRW 9.9T AI budget for 2026** and its target of **at least 50,000 government-led GPUs by 2028**. Budgets and targets differ from actual spending and installed capacity.
- It interprets the strategy as connecting domestic model development and talent programs with **public procurement, adoption vouchers, and consumer services** to create demand for the inference industry.

[Slide 172 · PDF page 173](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=173)

### Public compute needs separate measures for announcements, allocations, and usage

<Figure
  src="/articles/state-of-ai-2026/public-compute-access.png"
  alt="Comparison of public compute programs: the EU plans up to seven gigafactories around mid-2028; the UK allocated more than 3 million of 67 million requested GPU-hours; India approved 9.318 million GPU-hours for 237 projects. Actual usage is unreported in this comparison."
  caption="State of AI Report 2026, slide 173. The report distinguishes planned capacity, requests, approvals and allocations, and actual usage. Measured usage for the three programs is not reported in this comparison."
/>

- **EU:** up to seven gigafactories, targeting operation around mid-2028. **UK:** more than 3 million GPU-hours allocated against about 67 million requested by the initial SOV/AI cohort. **India:** the report classifies 9.318 million GPU-hours for 237 projects in an August update as approvals.
- **Electricity prices also change operating capacity.** A facility using 1 GW continuously for a year pays **$87.6M more annually** for every $0.01 increase per kWh. This is a simple continuous-use calculation, rather than an estimate for a particular data center contract.
- **Regulation and security form additional layers.** The report covers differences between US state rules and EU implementation schedules, controls on military use of models, and physical damage to cloud facilities. Comparisons of national obligations need to distinguish legal scope, timing, and the level of verification for each incident.

[Public compute: slide 173 · PDF page 174](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=174) · [Power costs: slide 177 · PDF page 178](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=178) · [Security: slides 166–168 begin at PDF page 167](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=167) · [Regulation: slides 183–185 begin at PDF page 184](https://www.stateof.ai/State-of-AI-Report-2026.pdf#page=184)

> Discussion question: To assess Korea’s AI sovereignty, which should we measure first: domestic model rankings, live GPUs, actual usage, or the ability to maintain operations with alternatives?
